BIOCHEMISTRY 201 — LECTURE 7: GLYCOLYSIS AND ITS REGULATION

1. OVERVIEW
Glycolysis is the cytosolic pathway converting one molecule of glucose into two
molecules of pyruvate. It is anaerobic, universal across domains of life, and
yields a net 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose. The pathway divides into an
investment phase (steps 1-5, consuming 2 ATP) and a payoff phase (steps 6-10,
producing 4 ATP).

2. THE INVESTMENT PHASE
Step 1: Hexokinase phosphorylates glucose to glucose-6-phosphate, consuming ATP.
  In hepatocytes this role is served by glucokinase, which has a much higher Km
  (~10 mM vs ~0.1 mM) and is not inhibited by its product.
Step 2: Phosphoglucose isomerase converts G6P to fructose-6-phosphate.
Step 3: Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) phosphorylates F6P to fructose-1,6-
  bisphosphate. This is the committed, rate-limiting step of the pathway.
Step 4: Aldolase cleaves F1,6BP into DHAP and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate.
Step 5: Triose phosphate isomerase interconverts DHAP and G3P.

3. THE PAYOFF PHASE
Step 6: Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase oxidises G3P and reduces NAD+
  to NADH, producing 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate.
Step 7: Phosphoglycerate kinase transfers a phosphate to ADP — the first
  substrate-level phosphorylation.
Step 8: Phosphoglycerate mutase moves the phosphate from C3 to C2.
Step 9: Enolase dehydrates 2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate.
Step 10: Pyruvate kinase transfers PEP's phosphate to ADP, yielding pyruvate.

4. REGULATION
PFK-1 is allosterically inhibited by ATP and citrate, signalling that the cell
is energy-replete. It is activated by AMP and, most potently, by fructose-2,6-
bisphosphate. That activator is produced by PFK-2, a bifunctional enzyme whose
kinase domain is active when dephosphorylated. Glucagon raises cAMP, activating
PKA, which phosphorylates PFK-2 and switches it to its phosphatase mode — so
glucagon suppresses hepatic glycolysis. Insulin does the reverse.

Hexokinase is inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate; glucokinase is not, which lets
the liver keep taking up glucose after a meal when other tissues are saturated.

Pyruvate kinase is activated by fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (feed-forward
activation) and inhibited by ATP and alanine.

5. CLINICAL RELEVANCE
Pyruvate kinase deficiency is the commonest enzymopathy of glycolysis and causes
a chronic haemolytic anaemia: erythrocytes have no mitochondria and depend
entirely on glycolysis for ATP. Affected cells show elevated 2,3-BPG, which
shifts the oxygen dissociation curve rightward and partly compensates.

Under anaerobic conditions, lactate dehydrogenase regenerates NAD+ from NADH by
reducing pyruvate to lactate — without which glycolysis would halt at step 6 for
want of oxidised cofactor.
